# Board summary: ChefJiw.ai

## The vote
**1 fund if, 2 pass.** Average score **3.3 / 10** (Product 4 FUND IF, Offers 3
PASS, Monopoly 3 PASS).

The earlier board, which saw only the idea, voted 3 pass at 2.3 / 10. This board
reviewed the app as built: one consumer cooking coach with a full cook mode, two
complete Thai recipes, AI questions mid-step, dish ideas from ingredients, a
daily AI price check, and Thailand, Japan and the US. All three agree the big cut
was made and the cook mode is shaped around the moment at the stove. All three
also agree the business is not proven yet, and that the scope has started to
widen again.

## Risks more than one member raised (most shared first)
1. **The one thing is not proven** (all three). The promise is that the cook gets
   the dish right. Two of six dishes are complete, nobody has cooked either from
   the app, and four say "coming soon". A wrong heat or time fails the cook at the
   moment the app exists for. Likelihood of success, in the Offers lens's terms,
   is zero today. → `/founder-launch`
2. **Focus is slipping again** (all three). Still no segment named in writing,
   and three surfaces were added while the core is a third done: dish ideas from
   ingredients (sorting six dishes when two can be cooked), the daily AI price
   check, and Japan and the US, which are separate markets with their own shops
   and prices. → `/founder-consumer`
3. **The paid tier sells the commodity** (Offers, Monopoly; Product on the
   missing purchase). Recipes and cook mode, the parts only this app has, are
   free. Members pay for unlimited AI questions, which a general AI assistant
   gives away. A cook making one dish a day may never pass 3 free uses. The 99 THB
   price came from an example range, and nobody can pay yet. → `/founder-offer`,
   `/founder-pricing`
4. **No way to reach cooks** (Monopoly, Product; Offers in its conditions). Not
   in the stores, no channel chosen, no cost to win a user measured.
   → `/founder-marketing`, `/founder-launch`
5. **Free substitutes and copycats** (Offers, Monopoly; Product asks the same
   question). A general AI assistant, a free cooking video or a ready-made meal is
   the real competition. The models are rented through OpenRouter, so any rival
   can rent them too, and nothing named stops a copycat in month six.
   → `/founder-competitors`

One risk from the earlier board is now smaller: AI cost per answer. Measured at
about US$0.00006 per typed answer and US$0.00016 per dish idea in small tests, it
will not sink a ~US$3 price on its own. Cost per active user, the daily price
job, store fees, hosting and the cost to win a user are still unmeasured.
→ `/founder-cfo`

## Conditions for a fresh vote (merged checklist)
- [ ] Name **one** segment in writing (one country, one language, one kind of
      cook), its size, and evidence that the pain is real and that they pay for
      help today. → `/founder-consumer`, `/founder-competitors` *(all)*
- [ ] Write the one sentence and the first five minutes, from the store page to
      the pan on the heat. *(Product)*
- [ ] Launch with the core: cook mode and finished dishes. Park the price check,
      Japan and the US, AI dish ideas and the sample filters until real cooks ask
      for them. *(Product, Monopoly)*
- [ ] Every listed dish complete and cooked from the app by people other than the
      founder, with the steps fixed afterwards. Nothing on screen says "coming
      soon" or "sample". *(all)*
- [ ] Proof at real stoves: a handful of cooks from the segment cook at home with
      the app, watched or recorded. Where they got stuck, what they asked, their
      taster score, and whether they cook again without a nudge. → `/founder-launch`
      *(all)*
- [ ] Store the step data (where cooks ask, where they stop, how it came out) and
      use it. Pick one durable advantage (tested-recipe know-how, step data or
      the Chef Elephant brand) and the number that shows it growing. *(Monopoly)*
- [ ] Map who serves that cook today and at what price, general AI assistants and
      free videos included. → `/founder-competitors` *(Monopoly, Offers)*
- [ ] A paid tier built on something a rival cannot give away free, around one
      measurable result (for example the taster score), with a name and a
      guarantee. → `/founder-offer` *(Offers, Monopoly)*
- [ ] A price set from that result and paid by real buyers, through connected
      in-app purchase or pre-sales; otherwise no paywall in the beta.
      → `/founder-pricing` *(Offers, Product)*
- [ ] One path with no seams: store page, onboarding, first dish, cook mode, the
      free limit (never a wall mid-step), purchase and receipt. LINE works or is
      not shown. One price per ingredient, not a sample price on the recipe and a
      daily AI price in the Market tab. *(Product)*
- [ ] Cost per active member (AI, the price job, store fees, hosting) against the
      price, and the cost to win a member through one tested channel to the first
      100 cooks. → `/founder-cfo`, `/founder-marketing`, `/founder-launch` *(all)*

Earlier conditions as they stand: **met**, the platform fits the cooking moment.
**Partly met**, one product (no segment, scope growing again); the first five
minutes (built, not written as one sentence for one cook); AI cost against price
(per answer only). **Open**, a price tied to a result, the competitor map, the
secret, the offer and guarantee, a tested channel, and proof from real buyers.

## Where the board disagrees
- **Fund if or pass.** The Product lens would fund once the core is proven: the
  cook mode is designed from the stove, the core is free, and the taster score
  closes the loop. The Offers and Monopoly lenses pass: the market and the paid
  tier are weak, and there is no secret and no distribution yet, however well
  the app is made.
- **Which cook.** The Product lens says the app's defaults (Thai, THB) already
  point at home cooks in Thailand. The Offers lens asks whether a cook abroad,
  who may not be able to buy the dish nearby, has the bigger pain. The Monopoly
  lens names Thai cooks living in Japan as one candidate for a small market to
  win first.
- **The price check.** The Product and Monopoly lenses would park it (cost per run
  unmeasured, AI prices can be wrong, any rival can run the same search). The
  Offers lens counts it as part of a broad stack, but asks whether buyers would
  miss it.
- **What members pay for.** The Offers lens: a result with a guarantee. The
  Monopoly lens: something only this company has, such as tested recipes, step
  data from real cooks, or the brand.

## The strongest version the board can see
This is narrower than what is built. **Chef Elephant gets your Thai dish right
at the stove:** cook mode for one named group of home cooks, with only dishes
that real cooks have cooked from the app and confirmed work. Cooking stays free.
The paid tier is tied to a result the cook can feel, measured by the taster
score, not to unlimited questions a free assistant already answers. The data
from real cooks (where they stop, what they ask, how the dish came out) becomes
the tested-recipe know-how a general AI assistant does not have, and the Chef
Elephant brand carries it. The price check, Japan and the US, and AI dish ideas
wait until cooks ask for them. The board has not settled the segment: home cooks
in Thailand, or Thai cooks abroad, with Japan as one candidate. Picking it is
the founder's first decision.
