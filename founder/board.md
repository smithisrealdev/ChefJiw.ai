# Board summary: ChefJiw.ai

## The vote
**3 pass.** Average score **2.3 / 10** (Offers 3, Monopoly 2, Product 2).

The board scored the idea as briefed: four products for four customer groups at
about 99 THB a month, with nothing built yet. All three see a fundable business
inside it. They disagree on which one.

## Risks more than one member raised (most shared first)
1. **No focus: four businesses pitched as one** (all three). A meal planner, a
   live cooking coach, a diet tracker and B2B restaurant software each have a
   different buyer, result, sales motion and price. No offer, competitive edge
   or first experience can be designed until one is chosen.
2. **The price may not cover the AI** (all three). It is unknown whether about
   99 THB a month covers inference per user, especially for voice/video
   coaching, let alone what it costs to win that user. → `/founder-cfo`
3. **Commodity against free substitutes** (Offers, Monopoly). Recipes from your
   fridge contents and AI coaching are already offered by general AI assistants,
   recipe sites and videos. Nothing named is ten times better, and nothing
   protects it from a copycat. → `/founder-competitors`
4. **No proof and no channel** (Offers, Monopoly). There are no users, no
   evidence that the coaching works or that the costing is accurate, and no
   chosen way to reach the first 100 customers. → `/founder-consumer`,
   `/founder-marketing`

## Conditions for a fresh vote (merged checklist)
- [ ] Pick **one** segment and **one** product; write down that the other three are parked.
- [ ] Describe it in one sentence with one customer, and write a walkthrough of the first five minutes. *(Product)*
- [ ] Choose the platform that fits that moment (web vs. mobile; hands-free voice if it is a cooking coach). *(Product)*
- [ ] Set a price tied to the result for that buyer, not one picked from an example range. → `/founder-pricing` *(Offers)*
- [ ] Show that the price covers AI inference cost per active user, with a payback on what it costs to win a customer. → `/founder-cfo` *(all)*
- [ ] Map who already serves that buyer and at what price; name the gap. → `/founder-competitors` *(Monopoly, Offers)*
- [ ] Name the secret or data advantage that a general AI assistant lacks. *(Monopoly)*
- [ ] Build an offer stack and guarantee around one measurable result. → `/founder-offer` *(Offers)*
- [ ] Show a tested channel to the first 100 customers, with the cost of each. → `/founder-marketing`, `/founder-launch` *(Monopoly)*
- [ ] Get early proof from a handful of real buyers in the chosen segment. → `/founder-consumer`, then `/founder-launch` *(Offers)*

## Where the board disagrees
- **Offers lens → the restaurant back-of-house tool.** Owners feel real pain
  (food cost, waste), have money to spend and are easy to reach, and menu costing
  is a result they can measure, so a result-based price and guarantee become
  possible.
- **Monopoly and Product lenses → Thai home cooking with the Chef Jiw persona,
  for Thai-food fans abroad.** It is a small market a focused brand can own,
  with one cuisine, one voice and a clear first five minutes. Monopoly adds that
  the restaurant tool could later build proprietary recipe and costing data.

## The strongest version the board can see
This is not what was pitched. **Chef Jiw, the Thai chef in your kitchen:** a
hands-free cooking coach that takes people abroad who love Thai food from
choosing a dish to plating it, in one authoritative voice. Diet tracking and
fridge-based meal planning are dropped, and restaurant tools wait until there is
a brand and a library of standardised Thai recipes to sell them on. The other
credible path is the reverse: a back-of-house costing tool for small Thai
restaurants, priced on the food-cost savings it proves. Pick one. Running both
from day one is what the board voted against.
