## What would flip a no

- If after the free dishes it clearly beat my YouTube routine, say the substitutes for ingredients I can't get here actually worked and tasted like what I ate in Thailand, I might pay for a month.
- If the free dishes showed me it fixes something YouTube can't, like actually telling me what to swap when my store has no holy basil and the dish still tasting like my takeaway.
- If a free dish actually tasted like my partner's family's cooking or the takeaway I love, clearly better than what ChatGPT gives me, I'd pay $2.99.
- If my free dishes actually turned out tasting authentic using only what my normal supermarket has, I'd pay the $2.99 without thinking about it.
- If the free dishes showed the voice coaching and substitutions for stuff I can't get without driving 30 minutes were clearly better than my chatbot, I'd pay the $2.99.
- If my partner or their family tried it and said the recipes actually taste like home, I'd probably cook more and upgrade.
- If after the free dishes the hands-free voice coaching actually got a real Thai dinner on the table in 20-25 minutes using what my normal supermarket stocks, noticeably better than a chatbot, I'd pay the $2.99.
- Knowing the recipes come from a named Thai home cook or chef I can check out, with a few real Thai users saying the dishes taste like home.
- If my first free dish genuinely tasted like my partner's family's cooking and it nudged me to cook again the next week, I'd pay the $2.99 without thinking.
- Knowing there's a real, named Thai home cook behind the recipes, and the free dishes actually tasting like the takeaway I love even with the substitutes.
- If the free dishes showed me the substitutes actually get close to what I ate in Thailand, and voice coaching felt like a real cook in my kitchen, I might pay the $2.99.
- If the free dishes actually tasted like what my partner's family makes, clearly better than a chatbot recipe, and the hands-free voice coaching really worked with messy hands at the stove.
