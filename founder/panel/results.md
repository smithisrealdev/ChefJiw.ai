## What would flip a no

- If the 99 baht got me lots of new family dinners I don't already know, each cheaper to make than buying the same from the stall, not just more questions to the chef.
- A Sunday meal-prep mode: family-size dishes I don't already know that keep in the fridge all week, with one shopping list, that saves me more than 99 baht a month at the market.
- A membership that unlocks dozens of everyday Thai dishes, not just more questions, for around 49 baht a month.
- Being able to ask the chef out loud by voice instead of typing, and even then not at 99 baht a month.
- Lots more dishes made for one pan in a tiny kitchen, so I'd cook with it most nights and actually run out of the free questions.
- Lots of new dishes I can't already cook, all doable in my one pan with cheap ingredients, so cooking at home actually beats buying a plate. More questions alone won't do it.
- A lot more cheap one-pan dishes made for a tiny kitchen, so it clearly saves me more on food each month than it costs.
- If the membership unlocked lots more cheap everyday dishes I don't already know how to cook, not just more questions.
- If I could just ask the chef out loud in Thai while I cook, without touching the phone, I might pay a little for that.
- If the membership came with lots of dishes I could batch-cook on Sunday for the whole week, not just two I already know. More questions alone will never make me pay.
- If the membership unlocked a lot more everyday dishes, like a new one every week, instead of just more questions.
- If the membership unlocked a lot of harder dishes I can't get right from a clip, like green curry or tom yum, instead of just more questions.
