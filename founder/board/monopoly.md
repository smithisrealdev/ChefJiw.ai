# Monopoly lens
Score: 2/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- There is a possible small market to own first: authentic Thai home cooking taught by a Thai chef persona, aimed at Thai-food fans abroad. A focused, culturally authentic brand is the one durable advantage the Monopoly lens sees within reach here.
- The restaurant back-of-house tools could, over time, build up proprietary data (standardised Thai recipes, costings) that a general AI chef would not have.
- Nothing is built yet, so narrowing it costs nothing.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No monopoly, four crowded markets at once.** Meal planning, cooking coaching, diet tracking and restaurant software are four separate markets, each likely served by funded incumbents and by general-purpose AI assistants the buyer may already use for free. Starting with a small share of four big markets is the opposite of what this lens looks for. Who else sells to each buyer is an open question for /founder-competitors.
2. **No proprietary technology or secret.** Recipes from your fridge contents and voice coaching are features general AI models already offer. Nothing in the brief is ten times better on something that matters, and nothing stops a copycat in month six. The brief names no secret: something the founder knows that others do not.
3. **No distribution, and the price may not pay for any.** No acquisition channel or platform has been chosen. At about 99 THB a month, it is unclear whether one paying user ever covers what it costs to acquire and serve them. CAC is a question for /founder-consumer and /founder-marketing; inference cost per user and payback are questions for /founder-cfo.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- If you could serve only one of the four segments, which would it be, and why would they pick you over what they use today?
- What do you know about Thai cooking, Thai restaurants or this buyer that a large AI company does not?
- How do your first 100 paying customers arrive, and what does each one cost to win?
- Why now? What has changed that makes this possible or needed today?
- What would still protect this business in ten years: network effects, data, scale or brand?
- Who is on the team, and what makes them right for the one segment you choose?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable (PASS). The idea could come back for a fresh vote if it narrows to one segment (the Thai-cuisine authority for cooks abroad looks the most ownable), names a credible secret or data advantage, and shows a tested channel to its first 100 customers along with what each costs.
