# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The scope has narrowed. Four products for four groups became one consumer cooking coach, with restaurant tools and diet tracking parked. Thai is the default language and THB the default currency, so a small market is in sight, though not yet chosen.
- The product is shaped around one moment, cooking at the stove: one step per screen, heat level, a timer that keeps running, the screen kept awake, big buttons, the step saved on exit. A general chat window is not built like this. The edge is real, though incremental.
- The app already sits on data a general AI assistant does not collect: which step of which recipe cooks ask about, where they stop, and how the dish came out (the taster score). The brief does not say whether this is stored and used. If it is, it is the most plausible seed of a lasting advantage.
- AI cost is measured, not guessed: about US$0.00006 per typed answer (five test questions) and US$0.00016 per dish idea (one test). At that rate a thousand questions cost about US$0.06 against a price of about US$3 a month, so AI cost per answer will not sink the margin. The models were picked by blind test and are reached through OpenRouter, so the app is not tied to one model maker.
- Chef Elephant and the taster mouse are the start of a brand, one of the four durable advantages the Monopoly lens looks for. At zero users it is only a character, but a character cooks trust is hard to copy.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nothing yet that a general AI assistant or a copycat cannot match, and the paid tier sells the most copyable part.** The intelligence is rented through OpenRouter, so any rival can rent the same models. Members pay for unlimited AI questions, the cheapest thing for anyone to give away. No secret is named, and the know-how that could become one is unproven: two complete recipes, neither test-cooked. Today nothing stops a copycat in month six, or a general assistant that adds a cook mode. When the product is a commodity, competition pushes the price toward the cost of an answer, which is close to zero.
2. **No way to reach cooks.** Not in the stores, no channel chosen, no cost to win a user measured, and no paying member possible until payment is built. The Monopoly lens counts distribution as half the business. What the app can afford to spend to win a member at about 99 THB a month depends on conversion and retention, and nobody has those numbers (/founder-cfo; the channel and the cost of the first 100 from /founder-marketing and /founder-launch).
3. **No small market to own; the effort is spread wide.** No segment in writing. The app serves cooks in Thailand and abroad, in two languages and three currencies, with a daily AI price job across three countries, while four of six dishes say "coming soon". The additions since the last review (dish ideas, the price check, more countries) widen the product instead of deepening one advantage, and each is easy to copy: the prices come from an AI web search any rival can run. A small player in three markets owns none of them.

The seven questions in brief: engineering, incremental; timing, cheap AI helps every rival, not this company in particular; monopoly, no small market chosen; people, team unknown; distribution, none; durability, nothing that holds yet; secret, not named.

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. Tonight a Thai home cook gets stuck halfway through basil pork. What do they do today, and why open this app instead of a general AI assistant they may already have? Who else serves this cook, and at what price? (/founder-competitors)
2. What do you know about cooking these dishes that a general assistant does not, and where does it come from? Who wrote the "questions people often ask" at each step: real cooks or a model?
3. Which one small group can you win nearly all of first, and how many are there? Thai cooks living in Japan, where yen prices and ingredient swaps may matter most, is one candidate. (Size and reach: /founder-consumer)
4. How do the first 100 cooks arrive, and what does each one cost? (/founder-marketing, /founder-launch)
5. What stops a copycat in month six? Pick the one advantage you are building (tested-recipe know-how, step data from real cooks, or the Chef Elephant brand) and the number that shows it growing.
6. If a rival gives AI questions away free, what is left for members to pay for? (/founder-pricing, /founder-offer)
7. Mid-step AI questions are typed. With wet hands, why is that better than an assistant the cook can talk to?
8. Why now for this company, and not just for AI cooking help in general?
9. Who will test the recipes, and who will own distribution?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Does not apply: the Monopoly lens votes PASS. Plainly, the app has moved well past the idea the last board saw, but on the Monopoly lens's questions little has changed. Today it is a well-made front end on rented models, with no named secret, no small market chosen and no way to reach cooks, within reach of a general AI assistant.

What would bring it back for a FUND IF:
1. One small market in one sentence: one country, one language, one kind of cook, and its size (/founder-consumer). Park the rest, including the other countries, and the price check unless it is shown to help that cook.
2. A map of who serves that cook today and at what price, general AI assistants and free cooking videos included (/founder-competitors).
3. The secret named and started: every live recipe test-cooked with real home cooks; step data (where they ask, where they stop, how it came out) stored and used; and one measured result a general assistant cannot match, such as the share of first-time cooks who rate the dish well (/founder-consumer).
4. A paid tier built on something a rival cannot give away free, at a price tied to a result (/founder-pricing, /founder-offer).
5. One channel tested to the first 100 cooks, with the cost per install and per paying member, and unit economics that include store fees and hosting (/founder-marketing, /founder-launch, /founder-cfo).

The earlier board's conditions. Met: the platform fits the cooking moment. Partly met: one product (but no segment, and the scope has grown again); the first five minutes (built, but not written as one sentence for one customer); covering AI cost (measured per answer; cost per active user, store fees, hosting and the cost to win a user are not). Open: a price tied to a result, the competitor map, the secret, the offer and guarantee, a tested channel, and proof from real buyers.
