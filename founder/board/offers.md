# Offers lens
Score: 3 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
The four value terms today: the dream outcome is modest (an everyday dish), the likelihood of getting it is near zero, the time to a result is short and the effort is low. The last two are real strengths.

- **Time:** a cook has a finished dish after one session, the same evening.
- **Effort:** one step per screen, the heat level, a timer that keeps running with a notification, a screen that stays awake, big buttons for messy hands, the step saved when the cook leaves, and the questions people often ask at that exact step.
- **The stack is already broad.** Most problems on the way to the dish have an answer: what to make from what is in the kitchen, what to buy and what it costs, why each amount, how to adapt the recipe (less salt, swap the pork), what to look for, help in a kitchen emergency, and a score at the end.
- **Delivery is cheap.** In tests a typed answer cost about US$0.00006 and a dish idea about US$0.00016, so the planned ~US$3 a month equals roughly 50,000 typed answers before store fees and hosting. That leaves room for a generous free tier, bonuses and a guarantee without losing money on AI. These are small samples, and the daily price job has not been measured.
- **Proof can be collected inside the product.** Once real cooks use the app, the taster score after cooking can gather the results this offer lacks.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **A weak market for the default buyer, and still no named buyer.** The default user is a Thai-speaking cook making everyday dishes such as basil pork, pork omelet and egg fried rice. For a cook in Thailand the pain may be small, and the alternatives are cheap or free: a recipe video, a general AI assistant, or buying the dish ready-made. The Offers lens checks four things: the pain is massive, buyers have money, they are easy to reach, and the market is growing. The brief shows none of them yet. Since the last review the app has spread to Japan and the US instead of narrowing, and three countries are three markets with different pains. Open: does this buyer pay for cooking help at all, how many such buyers are there, and what do they spend today (/founder-consumer, /founder-competitors).
2. **The paywall sells the commodity.** The parts only this app has, the recipes and cook mode, are free. A member pays only for unlimited AI questions, which a general AI assistant already answers for free. A cook making one dish a day may never need more than the 3 free AI uses, so there may be no reason to upgrade. The 99 THB price came from an example range, not from a result. Today nobody can pay at all: the paywall shows placeholders and in-app purchase is not connected. As built, the offer can only compete on being cheap. Open: how many questions a cook asks per session, and what keeps a member paying in month two (/founder-pricing, /founder-consumer).
3. **The likelihood of success is zero.** Neither complete recipe has been test-cooked, four of the six dishes say "coming soon", and the filters and shopping prices are sample values. There are no users, results, testimonials or guarantee. A cook whose first basil pork fails is unlikely to come back, and coaching someone through a recipe nobody has cooked invites exactly that failure. The Offers lens names likelihood as the place most offers fail, and this one has not started on it.

## The questions I would ask the founder
1. Who is the one buyer: a cook in Thailand, or a cook abroad (a Thai expat or a fan of Thai food) who may not be able to buy the dish nearby? Which has the bigger pain and the money to pay?
2. What does a member get that a free user does not, in one sentence a cook would repeat to a friend?
3. Who has cooked the two complete recipes from the app, and how did the dishes come out?
4. How many questions does a cook ask in one session? If it is fewer than three, why would anyone pay?
5. Why would this buyer open the app instead of watching a free video, asking a general AI assistant, or buying the dish ready-made?
6. What do you promise the cook, and what happens when the dish comes out wrong?
7. What would you have to add to charge about 200 THB and still be the obvious choice?
8. Would a buyer miss the daily price check if it were gone, and what does one run cost (/founder-cfo)?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
The vote is PASS, so there are no conditions for funding. Here is where the earlier board's conditions that matter to this lens stand:
- Pick one segment and one product: partly met. One product was picked, but not one segment.
- Show the price covers AI cost and the cost to win a user: partly met. AI cost per answer was measured, but not usage per user, store fees, hosting, the price job or the cost to win a user.
- Still open: a price tied to a result, an offer with a guarantee, a map of the alternatives, a tested channel, and proof from real buyers.

To come back for a fresh vote, show:

1. One buyer named in writing (one country, one kind of cook), with evidence that the pain is real and that they pay for help today (/founder-consumer, /founder-competitors).
2. The complete recipes cooked by real home cooks from that segment, with taster scores recorded and a handful of them saying it worked.
3. A paid offer built around one measurable result instead of "unlimited questions", with a clear name and a guarantee tied to that result, for example the taster score (/founder-offer).
4. A price set from that result and paid by real buyers, through connected in-app purchase or pre-sales (/founder-pricing).
5. Cost per active member (AI, the price job, store fees, hosting) set against the price, and the cost to win a member through a tested channel (/founder-cfo, /founder-marketing).
