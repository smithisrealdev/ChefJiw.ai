# Offers lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- One of the four segments passes the market test on paper: small owner-operated restaurants in Thailand have real pain (food cost, inconsistent recipes, ordering waste), spend money on their business, and are easy to find in person. Menu costing is a clear, measurable result.
- The Chef Jiw persona with authentic Thai cooking could be a distinctive name and identity for an offer, not just "another recipe app".
- Freemium lets buyers try it with no risk, which makes them believe it works more quickly.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No single market, so no offer.** Four buyers with four different dream outcomes (dinner tonight, authentic Thai abroad, lower food cost, hitting macros) cannot share one stack, one guarantee or one message. A stack built for everyone persuades no one.
2. **Competing on price in a low-pain market.** The business is defined by a ~99 THB price picked from an example range, aimed mostly at consumers. For a home cook the pain is mild and free substitutes (recipe sites, videos, general AI chatbots) are everywhere. At that price nothing is "too good to refuse". It is a commodity, and inference cost per user could exceed the price. That cost is an open question for /founder-cfo.
3. **Likelihood is zero today.** No product, no users, no proof that the coaching works or that costing is accurate. The value equation fails on perceived likelihood before effort is even scored.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- If you could serve only one of the four segments, which one has the most pain and the most money? Can you name ten real buyers in it?
- What result can you promise in the first week, and what guarantee would you put behind it (for a restaurant, say, "find X THB of food-cost savings or it is free")?
- Why would a restaurant believe an AI's costing numbers? What proof would you show first?
- What does one active user cost to serve in AI inference, and does 99 THB cover it? (/founder-cfo)
- Who else sells this to the same buyer, and at what price? (/founder-competitors)
- Would buyers in that one segment pay double and still see this as the obvious choice? (/founder-consumer)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable (PASS). What would change the vote: narrow to one segment (the restaurant back-of-house tool is the strongest candidate), set a price tied to the result rather than to an example range, build a stack and guarantee around that one outcome, and show early proof from a handful of real buyers.
