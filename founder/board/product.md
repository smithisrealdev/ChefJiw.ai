# Product lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- Cook mode is designed from the moment at the stove, which is what the Product lens asks for. One step per screen with what to do, what to look for and what the step did; the heat level; a timer that keeps running with a notification; a screen that stays awake; big buttons for messy hands; the step saved if the cook leaves. A phone in the kitchen fits that moment, so the earlier "choose the platform" condition looks met.
- "Ask the chef" is in the right order for a cook mid-step: the questions people usually ask at that step, then the typed AI box, then fire and burns.
- The biggest cut has been made. Restaurant tools and diet tracking were not built. There is one product: a consumer cooking coach.
- The core is free. Every recipe and the whole cook mode cost nothing, so a new cook can finish a dish before anyone asks for money.
- The loop closes: after cooking, the app asks how the dish came out. That answer is the right measure of the one thing.
- There is care in parts nobody sees: the AI allowance is counted on the server, the models were picked in a blind test on the app's own prompts, each stored price keeps its source, and each ingredient gives a one-line reason for its amount.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **The one thing is not proven.** The promise is that the cook gets the dish right. Two recipes are complete and nobody has cooked either one from the app. A wrong heat level or time fails the cook at the exact moment the app exists for, and they will blame the app. A cook who likes it runs out of dishes after two dinners, and four of the six marked "coming soon" tell a first-time user the product is unfinished. Whether cooks come back is unknown (/founder-launch: a small beta measures it).
2. **Focus is slipping again.** Since the earlier review the product has added three surfaces while the core is a third done: dish ideas from ingredients, a daily AI price check, and Japan and US support. Each is a second product with its own ways to fail. "What do I have?" sorts six dishes when two can be cooked. The price job runs every day on a model whose cost per run is not measured, and prices found by AI web search can be wrong. Japan and the US serve cooks abroad, with other shops and prices than a cook in Thailand, and no segment is named in writing. That is why the pitch takes a paragraph instead of a sentence.
3. **Cook mode is owned; the experience around it is not.** There is no store listing and no chosen channel, so the first touchpoint does not exist. The paywall shows placeholders and purchase is not connected, so there is no sale and no receipt. LINE sign-in does not work, and the filters are labelled as sample values. Inside the app the seams show: the recipe page uses sample prices while the Market tab shows daily AI prices, so one ingredient can carry two numbers; AI dish ideas point past the two recipes cook mode can guide; and nobody has yet asked the chef a question at a real stove, where a slow or wrong answer can cost the dish. The cost to win a user is unknown (/founder-marketing).

## The questions I would ask the founder

1. In one sentence: which cook, which dish, and what does "it worked" mean to them?
2. Walk me through that cook's first five minutes, from the store page to the pan on the heat. How many screens and taps? Must they sign in before they can cook?
3. If you could launch only cook mode and two dishes, what would the cook miss? What goes first: the price check, Japan and the US, AI dish ideas or the filters?
4. Who outside the team has cooked basil pork with the app from start to finish, and where did they stop or ask a question? Who checks that an adapted recipe (less salt, pork swapped out) still works?
5. What does the cook see when they want a fourth AI answer in the middle of a step, with the wok hot?
6. When the AI suggests a dish that is not one of the six, what does the cook do next? When an answer is slow, the network drops, or the answer is wrong about heat or doneness, what happens?
7. Which price should the cook trust, the sample price on the recipe page or the daily AI price, and who checks the daily prices? What does one daily run cost? (/founder-cfo)
8. With a general AI chat app or a cooking video on the same phone, where exactly does Chef Elephant do better, step by step? (/founder-competitors)
9. After a cook has made both complete dishes, what brings them back tomorrow?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

As it stands I would not fund it. I would vote FUND when all four of these are true:

1. **One cook, one sentence, one scope.** Name the segment in writing (/founder-consumer); the app's defaults, Thai and THB, already point at one. Write the sentence and the first five minutes. Launch with cook mode only: hide the price check, Japan and the US, AI dish ideas and the sample filters until the core works, and bring each back only when real cooks ask for it.
2. **Every listed dish works.** Each dish on the list is complete and has been cooked from the app by people other than the founder, with the steps fixed afterwards. Anything else comes off the list. Nothing on screen says "coming soon" or "sample".
3. **Proof at real stoves.** A handful of cooks from that segment cook a dish at home with the app, watched or recorded: where they got stuck, what they asked, how they answered the taster mouse, and whether they cook again without a nudge (/founder-launch).
4. **One path with no seams.** Store page, onboarding, first dish, cook mode, free limit, purchase and receipt work end to end. Either in-app purchase is connected at a real price (/founder-pricing) or there is no paywall in the beta. The free limit never lands as a wall in the middle of a step. LINE works or is not shown.

The earlier board's conditions as they stand now: choosing the platform is met. One segment and one product is partly met (one product, but no segment in writing and three new surfaces). One sentence and the first five minutes is partly met (the five minutes exist in the app; the sentence does not). AI cost against price is partly met (cost per answer is measured in small tests; the price job, store fees, hosting and the cost to win a user are not: /founder-cfo, /founder-marketing). A price tied to a result, the competitor map, the secret a general AI assistant lacks, the offer and guarantee, a tested channel and early proof are open.
