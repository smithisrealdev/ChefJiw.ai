# Product lens
Score: 2 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The Thai home-cooking persona is the one part that could be a product. "Learn to cook real Thai food at home, with a Thai chef in your kitchen" fits in one sentence, has a clear first five minutes (pick a dish, get coached through it), and has a natural owner of taste: one cuisine, one voice, one standard of "authentic".
- Nothing is built yet, so cutting costs nothing. That is the best time to say no.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus.** The founder chose all four products and all four customers when asked for one. That is at least three businesses: a consumer cooking app, a diet tracker, and B2B restaurant software (costing, inventory, ordering) with a different buyer, sales motion, workflow and price, and no price set yet. The Product lens asks what to cut until one thing is left; today the answer is "nothing", and the experience cannot be designed until it is.
2. **No describable first experience.** A busy cook in Bangkok, a Thai-food fan abroad, a café owner doing stock counts and a macro tracker open the product for different reasons, in different settings (kitchen with messy hands, back office, gym). Working back from any one of those moments gives a different product, platform and onboarding. Web vs. app is undecided, and for a hands-busy cooking coach that choice is the experience, not a detail.
3. **Seams everywhere.** Fridge-to-recipe needs reliable ingredient input; live coaching needs voice/video that works mid-cook; diet use needs trustworthy nutrition data; restaurants need data that touches money. Each is a hand-off between the user, the AI and outside data where a wrong answer breaks trust. Spread four ways, none gets the craft it needs. Whether ~99 THB a month covers voice/video AI use is unknown (an open question for /founder-cfo).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, with one customer: what is ChefJiw?
- Describe the first five minutes for that one customer: where they are standing, what they tap, what they cook.
- Which three of the four products would you drop today, and why are you keeping the fourth?
- Why does it have to be one product rather than a Thai cooking coach first and restaurant tools later or never?
- Would your chosen customer actually pay to cook with it, and at what price? (open question for /founder-consumer)
- Who already does this well for that customer? (open question for /founder-competitors)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable (PASS). This vote would be reconsidered if the founder comes back with one product for one customer, a written first-five-minutes walkthrough, a platform choice that fits it, and the other three ideas explicitly parked.
